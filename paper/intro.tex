\thesischapter{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}



After twenty six years of the euro was introduced in 1999, there are still six members of European Union retain their own currency.
The Czech Republic, Denmark, Hungary, Poland, Romania and Sweden belong to the single market.
They can trade freely with the euro area and are restricted by the same regulatory framework as it members.
However they each retain an independent monetary policy and an exchange rate moves against the euro.
There are different reasons why they are keeping their own national currency.
The reasons for each case is different but the result is the same.
The six countries are integrated with the euro area in trade while continuing to carry exchange rate risk against it.
The main question this thesis ask whether carrying the risk has cost them anything in growth.

\vspace{\baselineskip}

The question has been studied for decades with unsettled answer.
The disagreement is not only about the size of the effect.
Part of the reason is that the underlying theory does not predict one effect.
It predicts a relationship that changes with conditions which means a single average estimate for a group of countries can be consistent with any finding.
The other part is that exchange rate volatility is not an observation.
It has to be constructed.
When it constructs differently will arrive at different conclusions.

\vspace{\baselineskip}

This thesis approaches the question with a smaller sample and more specific measure.
It starting point is Morina et al. (\citeyear{morina_effect_2020}) study and its the closest to our study.
It estimates the effect of exchange rate volatility on growth across fourteen Central and Eastern European countries.
They find the trade mechanism is the least conclusive.
This paper covers only six European members that kept their own currencies for the whole of the study period from 2005 to 2024.
And volatility is measured against the euro instead of against a basket of trading partners.
It is following the specification from Latief and Lefen (\citeyear{latief_effect_2018}).
It is estimated from daily data which allows it to vary from year to year instead of summarizing as a single figure.
The relationship is allowed to depend on how much each country trades with the euro.
This is what the theory says it should depend on and what Morina et al. (\citeyear{morina_effect_2020}) leave unresolved.
Two research questions follow:
\vspace{\baselineskip}

\textbf{Does exchange rate volatility against euro influence economic growth in the six non-euro European member states?}

\vspace{\baselineskip}

\textbf{Does the effect of volatility on growth depend on the extent of country's trade integration with euro area?}

\vspace{\baselineskip}

The six countries fit this question.
They are alike in the way that make comparison possible.
The shared one market and one dominant trading partner and same shocks over twenty years.
And they also have differences.
Some let their currency move freely and some close the euro.
And some have large trade intensity in euro area while others do not.
This is the reason why the conditional relationship should be tested rather than assumed.
Three hypotheses follow from the above and are tested in the empirical part of this thesis:

\vspace{\baselineskip}
\textbf{H1:} Exchange rate volatility against the euro has no independent relationship with
real GDP growth in the six non-euro EU members.

\vspace{\baselineskip}

\textbf{H2:} The effect of exchange rate volatility on growth declines as euro area trade intensity rises.
\vspace{\baselineskip}

\textbf{H3:} The relationship between exchange rate volatility and growth is detectable only
in periods when volatility differs across the six countries.

\vspace{\baselineskip}
The remaining part of this thesis is structured as follows.
The Chapter 1 set out the theoretical framework and literature review.
Chapter 2 presents the history of European monetary integration and examination.
Chapter 3 describes the data and methodology.
In Chapter 4 reports the empirical results and discussion.
Chapter 5 discusses the limitations and concludes.
